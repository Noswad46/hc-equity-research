The universe is curated rather than comprehensive. Some exclusions are a matter
of scope, but one is a deliberate judgement about comparability and is worth
stating plainly, because it removes companies a reader would reasonably expect to
find here.

## IFRS filers are excluded

GSK, AstraZeneca, Sanofi and BioNTech are not covered. All four report under
IFRS, and two separate problems follow from that.

**The accounting basis differs in a way that changes the metrics.** Under IAS 38,
development costs that meet the capitalisation criteria are carried on the
balance sheet as an intangible asset and amortised over the life of the product.
US GAAP, with narrow exceptions, requires essentially all research and
development to be expensed as incurred. The same underlying spending therefore
lands in different places in the accounts.

Two of the metrics on this site are built directly on that line:

- **R&D intensity** would be systematically understated for an IFRS filer, since
  part of what a US filer expenses never reaches the IFRS income statement.
- **Cash runway** is computed from operating cash flow, and capitalised
  development costs are typically classified as investing rather than operating
  outflows, which flatters operating cash flow on the same spending.

Ranking an IFRS filer against a US GAAP filer on either measure would produce a
number that looks comparable and is not. There is no adjustment available from
the data this site uses that would put them on the same footing.

**Several do not file quarterly with the SEC.** As foreign private issuers they
file an annual 20-F rather than 10-Qs. Every figure here is built from discrete
quarterly periods, and for these companies those periods do not exist in the SEC
data at all — there is nothing to derive them from.

Covering them properly means mapping the IFRS concepts, sourcing interim
reporting from outside EDGAR, and presenting them as a separate, clearly labelled
comparison rather than as rows in the same ranked table. That is a deliberate
piece of work, not a configuration change, and it has not been done.

## What this means when reading the site

Where a subsector has a meaningful share of its industry reporting under IFRS,
the ranked tables show the US GAAP-reporting part of that industry, not the
industry. The vaccines and infectious disease pages carry a specific note to that
effect, because the gap there is large enough to change how the page should be
read.
